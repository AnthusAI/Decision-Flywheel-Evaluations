Classify the monetary policy stance of this sentence from US Federal Reserve (FOMC) communication: hawkish (points toward tighter policy, such as higher rates or fighting inflation), dovish (points toward easier policy, such as lower rates or supporting growth), or neutral (mixed, no change, or not about policy).

ANNOTATION GUIDE (excerpt): Shah, Paturi and Chava (2023), "Trillion Dollar Words: A New Financial Dataset, Task & Market Analysis", ACL 2023, Section 3 and Appendix C.2 (Table 10). Published at aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.368 (CC BY 4.0).

Label definitions. Dovish sentences were any sentence that indicates future monetary policy easing. Hawkish sentences were any sentence that would indicate a future monetary policy tightening. Neutral sentences were those with mixed sentiment, indicating no change in the monetary policy, or those that were not directly related to monetary policy stance.

The guide is built by dividing each target sentence into defined categories. Category definitions:
- Economic Status: A sentence pertaining to the state of the economy, relating to unemployment and inflation.
- Dollar Value Change: A sentence pertaining to changes such as appreciation or depreciation of value of the United States Dollar on the Foreign Exchange Market.
- Energy/House Prices: A sentence pertaining to changes in prices of real estate, energy commodities, or energy sector as a whole.
- Foreign Nations: A sentence pertaining to trade relations between the United States and a foreign country. If not discussing United States we label neutral.
- Fed Expectations/Actions/Assets: A sentence that discusses changes in the Fed yields, bond value, reserves, or any other financial asset value.
- Money Supply: A sentence that overtly discusses impact to the money supply or changes in demand.
- Key Words/Phrases: A sentence that contains key word or phrase that would classify it squarely into one of the three label classes, based upon its frequent usage and meaning among particular label classes.
- Labor: A sentence that relates to changes in labor productivity.

A label of Dovish, Hawkish or Neutral is assigned based on the contents of each sentence by category (Table 10):

Economic Status
 Dovish: when inflation decreases, when unemployment increases, when economic growth is projected as low
 Hawkish: when inflation increases, when unemployment decreases, when economic growth is projected high, when economic output is higher than potential supply/actual output, when economic slack falls
 Neutral: when unemployment rate or growth is unchanged, maintained, or sustained
Dollar Value Change
 Dovish: when the dollar appreciates
 Hawkish: when the dollar depreciates
 Neutral: N/A
Energy/House Prices
 Dovish: when oil/energy prices decrease, when house prices decrease
 Hawkish: when oil/energy prices increase, when house prices increase
 Neutral: N/A
Foreign Nations
 Dovish: when the US trade deficit decreases
 Hawkish: when the US trade deficit increases
 Neutral: when relating to a foreign nation's economic or trade policy
Fed Expectations/Actions/Assets
 Dovish: Fed expects subpar inflation, Fed expecting disinflation, narrowing spreads of treasury bonds, decreases in treasury security yields, and reduction of bank reserves
 Hawkish: Fed expects high inflation, widening spreads of treasury bonds, increase in treasury security yields, increase in TIPS value, increase bank reserves
 Neutral: N/A
Money Supply
 Dovish: money supply is low, M2 increases, increased demand for loans
 Hawkish: money supply is high, increased demand for goods, low demand for loans
 Neutral: N/A
Key Words/Phrases
 Dovish: when the stance is "accommodative", indicating a focus on "maximum employment" and "price stability"
 Hawkish: indicating a focus on "price stability" and "sustained growth"
 Neutral: use of phrases "mixed", "moderate", "reaffirmed"
Labor
 Dovish: when productivity increases
 Hawkish: when productivity decreases
 Neutral: N/A
